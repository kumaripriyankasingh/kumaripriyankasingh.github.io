# The Silence Between the Stories

I haven't written here in a long time. If you work in tech you probably already know why. AI is eating all of us, and I don't entirely mean that as a complaint.

It feels like we spent our whole lives carrying questions we couldn't answer, and now there's something that will answer all of them, instantly, at 2am, without ever getting tired of us. So we've all decided to fix our lives first and get back to living afterwards - which is a bit like refusing to open the box until you've read the entire manual, except the box is your life and the manual is being written by something that has never once been left on read.

Anyway. Something has been going on in my head since yesterday and I need it out of my system, so here it is.

I was walking with a friend yesterday. We had about thirty minutes, and we ended up doing that thing where you summarise yourself out loud. This is how I am. This is how I react. This is what I'm like in relationships, this is what I don't handle well. He gave his version. I gave mine.

I have since spent considerably more time reviewing those thirty minutes than we spent having them. Somewhere around the fourth replay I stopped being an overthinker and became a small unfunded research institute.

And what the research has concluded is this: neither of us was telling the truth.

Not lying. The sentences were just too small. I said "I'm someone who…" and even while it was leaving my mouth some part of me knew it wasn't quite right. He said something about himself and I remember thinking, that's not you, that's just the sentence you've settled on.

Here's the thing I keep circling.

Our entire species runs on stories. That's the whole trick, as far as I can tell. Money is a story. Countries are a story. Companies are a story that a few thousand people agree to show up to every morning. None of it exists anywhere except in the gap between people who've decided to believe the same thing. We conquered the planet because we can say something to a stranger and have them act on it.

And then, inevitably, we turned that technology on ourselves.

## The Narrator in the Head

Because "I'm an overthinker" is also a story. So is "I'm bad at closeness," "I don't really get attached." I have a narrator in my head whose entire job is to take three decades of noise and edit it into something with a plot, and honestly the narrator is good. Great instincts. Excellent at spin. The only problem is that it wasn't there for most of what happened. It's a PR person writing up an event it did not attend, from photographs, under deadline.

So when I describe myself to someone in thirty minutes, I'm not handing them me. I'm handing them the press release.

And the truly efficient part is that I believe it too. I'm not deceiving him.

## Language Is a Terrible Translator

Then there's the deeper problem, which is language itself.

Language did not evolve so that one person could accurately transmit their interior to another. It evolved so a group could coordinate - where the food is, who's not to be trusted, what we're doing about the thing tomorrow. It is a magnificent instrument for organising a hunt, a religion, or a product launch. It is a spectacularly bad instrument for the one job we actually want it for.

So words are the biggest liars, and not because we're being dishonest. It's the opposite. It's that we're trying so hard. You can build the most careful sentence of your life and it still arrives smaller than it left. "I'm fine" is the most load-bearing sentence in the language. I have said it when the honest answer would have taken a year to explain.

## The New Thing in the Room

Which brings me back to AI.

It has read every self-description ever written. Every diary, every confession, every unsent letter typed at 3am and deleted at 3:04. It knows the shape of a human being better than any human has ever known it. Ask it who you are and it will tell you, fluently, with structure, possibly with bullet points, and it will sound more true than anything you managed to say out loud on a road with a friend.

It can narrate me better than I can narrate me. It just can't be me while it does it.

Because it's still narrating. Same trick my head does, only faster and with better sources. And the one thing it has no access to - the thing nobody has access to - is what was actually happening in me while I was walking and talking and carefully not saying the real thing. That never got written down. It has no text. It isn't in the training data because it was never data.

That part is the only part that was ever mine.

## What We Actually Want

We all want to be heard. Not by everyone - by one particular person, in whose company we stop performing. And we go about it by talking, which is the worst available tool for the job. Maybe that's why the people we fall for were never the ones who liked what we said. They're the ones who understood the silence around it and didn't ask for a translation.

So. Two things I'm taking from this.

## What I Want to Do Differently

I want to be more careful when I begin a sentence with "I am." I don't think I know. I think I have a very well-rehearsed guess that I've stopped auditing.

And when someone talks to me, I want to stop drafting my reply while they're still speaking. I do this constantly - someone tells me something and I'm already assembling the advice, or the similar thing that happened to me once, which is really just waiting for my turn with extra steps. Most of the time they aren't asking to be explained. They want a witness while they say it out loud.

That much I can do.

And it's the one thing you can't ask AI for.
